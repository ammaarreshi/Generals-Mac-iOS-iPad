# Research: Irregular Adversaries and Cross-Cutting Battlefield Trends (2024-2026)

Purpose: design input for a Zero Hour mod that modernizes the GLA and adds cross-faction technology effects. Scope is public, open-source, capability-level. It contains no construction, tasking, or operational how-to. Compiled 22 Oct 2026 from web sources; confidence flags are marked inline. "Unverified" means a single secondary source, a party's own claim, or an aggregator.

## 1. Overview

The defining change since 2024 is that cheap, mass-produced, networked sensors and munitions are no longer a state monopoly. Commercial quadcopters, FPV drones, fiber-optic drones, one-way attack drones, uncrewed boats and anti-ship missiles give irregular forces a stand-off precision strike and ISR capability that used to need an air force. The Red Sea campaign, the HTS drone arm in Syria, JNIM in the Sahel, Myanmar's resistance and Mexican cartels all show this. Against that, irregulars stay limited by supply chains, leadership exposure, industrial depth, and states' ability to adapt. Myanmar's resistance lost its drone edge by 2025, and Hezbollah's older arsenal was largely destroyed. The cross-cutting lessons from Ukraine, Israel-Iran, the Red Sea and India-Pakistan are that the front is a drone-saturated "kill zone", that EW and spoofing are everywhere, that missile defense loses the cost-exchange fight unless cheap interceptors scale, and that concealment, dispersion and decoys matter more than armor. Because many 2026 items surfaced only in secondary reporting, they are flagged "unverified" where relevant.

## 3. Part B: Cross-cutting trends 2024-2026

1. **FPV dominance and the kill zone.** FPVs became the leading cause of destroyed armor and a large share of casualties. One study estimated about 65% of Russian tank losses by early 2025 (secondary). Syrskyi put the frontline kill zone at 10 km in Oct 2025, and a drone-forces commander said 25 km or more in May 2026 (Euromaidan Press, Ukrainska Pravda). Russian infiltration shifted to groups of two or three. Source: Euromaidan Press (Oct 2025, Jul 2026).
2. **Fiber-optic drones defeat EW.** Appeared from mid-2024 and are immune to RF jamming. They trade range (about 2-20 km) and weight for resilience, and no cheap universal counter exists. Countermeasures mix nets, shotguns, lasers, and IR/acoustic detection. Source: Euromaidan Press (Feb 2025); Photonics Online (Mar 2025).
3. **EW and jamming saturation; GPS spoofing.** Drones respond with dual-mode links and better antennas. Russia reportedly moved from 16- to 32-element anti-jam antennas on Shahed-type drones. GPS jamming and spoofing spilled into NATO airspace (first Norwegian detection Jan 2025). Source: Liga.net (2025); ArcticToday.
4. **Interceptor drones.** Ukraine's Sting-class interceptors cost about $2,000-2,500 each versus $20-50 k for a Shahed. Reports say about 70% of Shahed kills in Jan 2026 (1,704 total) came from interceptor drones (Economist via DroneXL, secondary; hit-rate claims 60-90% are unverified). Source: DroneXL (Mar 2026); Unmanned Airspace.
5. **Unmanned ground vehicles.** UGVs shifted from experimental to routine logistics, casevac, and some kamikaze use. Ukraine reportedly delivered about 15,000 in 2025 versus 2,000 in 2024, and some brigades run 80-90% of resupply by UGV (brigade claims, unverified). Source: Modern War Institute (West Point); Lowy Interpreter.
6. **Drone motherships.** Russia converted a Molniya one-way drone into a carrier launching an FPV near the front, extending reach to roughly 40 km and relaying the link. Ukraine has similar concepts. Source: Euromaidan Press (Jun 2025); Pravda (Sep 2025).
7. **AI targeting and terminal guidance.** Final-approach autonomy lets an FPV finish a strike after link loss. Vendors supplied tens of thousands of kits in 2025 (50,000 Auterion/SkyFall Shrike FPVs; 33,000 Pentagon-funded guidance kits). Value is debated and vendor claims are unverified. Source: Kyiv Post; RealClearDefense (Jun 2025).
8. **Glide bombs.** Russian guided-bomb launches reached roughly 3,500/month in early 2025 and 5,000+ by late 2025 (Ukrainian General Staff data). Figures for 2026 (8,000+/month) come from weaker sources and are unverified. They crush fixed positions and cities from beyond air-defense range. Source: JAPCC; Militarnyi.
9. **Decline of massed armor.** Cope cages, turtle tanks and drone nets spread since 2023, with limited effect against top-attack and side attacks and mostly psychological value per experts. Armor survives mainly through dispersion, small groups, and EW. Source: Business Insider; Kyiv Post.
10. **Counter-UAS beyond guns: lasers and HPM.** Israel's Iron Beam interim system downed Hezbollah drones from Oct 2024, at about $2.50-3.50 per shot against a roughly $50 k Tamir. Limits include weather, magazine depth, line of sight. Epirus's Leonidas HPM reportedly disabled 61 of 61 drones including a 49-drone swarm in a company-run test (Aug 2025, unverified independently). Source: TWZ (29 May 2025); Epirus press release.
11. **Missile-defense cost-exchange and stockpile exhaustion.** In the June 2025 Israel-Iran war, about 550 Iranian ballistic missiles were fired and the US reportedly used over 150 THAAD and about 80 SM-3 interceptors. No new US THAAD deliveries since Jul 2023 (CSIS). SM-3 costs roughly $9-24 M, THAAD about $15 M. Source: CSIS, Rumbaugh (5 Dec 2025).
12. **ISR transparency and "nowhere to hide".** Cheap thermal and night-vision drones shortened the kill chain from hours to minutes, and thermal cloaks sometimes create cold spots. Concealment, dispersion and weather still create windows (not literally transparent). Source: SOFREP (2026); Euromaidan Press (May 2025).
13. **Dispersion, decoys, deception.** Russia builds decoys at a scale similar to its strike Shaheds (about 2,500/month claimed). India and Pakistan both used cheap drones as decoys and loitering munitions, and analysts judged loitering munitions poor at suppressing air defenses (poor drone-to-system ratio). Source: HUR via Pravda; SSPC/IDSA (2025).
14. **Operation Spiderweb (1 Jun 2025).** Ukraine's SBU used drones hidden in trucks near five Russian airbases. The SBU claimed 41 aircraft; OSINT confirmed far fewer (about 12-20, with Estonian intelligence citing at least 7 bombers destroyed). A fifth strike failed when a truck caught fire. Shows that parked aircraft deep inside a state are vulnerable to cheap, pre-positioned systems. Source: Janes; OSINT reporting; Estonian intelligence.
15. **Subterranean and urban warfare remain slow.** Tunnels absorbed bombing, and clearing needed layered detection, robots, dogs, foam and demolition. Source: CALL, Andrews (Sep 2025).

## 4. Signature strengths and vulnerabilities of irregular forces vs a state

Strengths:
- Cost asymmetry: $10-20 k Houthi attacks forced $1-2 M intercepts, and the US Navy's spend topped $1 B (CTC Sentinel).
- Dispersed, hard-to-find production in houses, garages, and workshops (HTS).
- Speed of adoption: commercial drones modified within months, with state-style propaganda (JNIM).
- Tolerance of attrition: Houthis survived a 52-day air campaign with over 800 reported targets hit (CTC Sentinel, CENTCOM figures vary).
- Terrain and population cover: tunnels and urban terrain (Gaza), mountains (Yemen).
- Political and information leverage: shipping disruption and rally-around-the-flag effects.
- Access to dual-use commercial tech and satellite comms (Starlink).

Vulnerabilities:
- Dependence on imported components and external patrons (CAR; Myanmar costs doubled).
- Leadership and communications exposure (Hezbollah pagers; Israeli strikes on commanders).
- Weak air defense against a determined state air campaign, with loss of stockpiles (Hezbollah's rockets fell from ~100k to ~25k, an unverified estimate).
- Simple RF links, few frequency channels, easy for state jammers (Myanmar military jammers beat resistance drones).
- Limited ability to hold ground or govern, since militias that must govern are most vulnerable (Johnsen).
- Scale limits: cannot match state industrial output over time (Myanmar military closed the drone gap in 2025).
- Visible launch signatures and reliance on fixed infrastructure that ISR can find.
