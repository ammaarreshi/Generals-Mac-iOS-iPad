Compiled 2026-10-22 from open web sources. Public, capability-level only. Most parade hardware was shown statically and is not combat-proven; many designations are analyst guesses. Confidence tags: **[official]** state-announced name, **[reported]** consistent open-source reporting, **[unverified]** single-source or speculative, **[background]** long-fielded system not re-checked against a 2025-26 source in this pass.

## 1. Overview: PLA character in 2025-26

The PLA is a large, increasingly high-end force built around "informationized" and now "intelligentized" joint warfare: win by destroying the adversary's operational system (sensors, networks, bases, logistics) rather than attriting its units one by one. Its core is a mass of precision fires (the world's largest conventional ballistic/cruise missile force, including the leading hypersonic arsenal per the Pentagon) behind an A2/AD "counter-intervention" shell, plus rapid adoption of unmanned and counter-unmanned systems. The Pentagon's December 2025 report puts operational nuclear warheads in the low 600s with more than 1,000 expected by 2030, more than 100 new silos probably loaded with solid-fuel ICBMs, and a launch-on-warning posture. The April 2024 abolition of the Strategic Support Force into an Aerospace Force, a Cyberspace Force and an Information Support Force (plus a promoted Joint Logistics Support Force) shows information/space/cyber now sit directly under the Central Military Commission. The Pentagon says the PLA expects to be able to "fight and win" a Taiwan war by end-2027, and describes four campaigns: coercion, joint firepower strike, blockade, and island landing (the landing being the hardest). Its industrial base is the other pillar: CSIS and others describe a defense and shipbuilding base on a near-wartime footing, with shipbuilding capacity estimated at roughly 230x the US (ONI-derived). Weaknesses are equally structural: no recent combat experience, a purge-hit senior leadership (including the Rocket Force), weak amphibious lift/logistics, and heavy reliance on networks and space sensors that a peer would attack. Post-2026 Chinese commentary on the US-Israel war with Iran stresses cheap-drone vs expensive-interceptor cost exchange, distributed kill webs, and attacks on ports, airfields, power and data centers.

## 3. Signature strengths and vulnerabilities (design hooks vs the US)

**Strengths**
- **Mass precision fires.** Rocket artillery (PCH-191) plus MRBMs/IRBMs/ASBMs deliver volume saturation from the rear; cheap salvos meant to overwhelm defenses and hit exposed forces and bases.
- **Missile ladder from tactical to strategic.** Guided rockets, DF-17/26/27 and YJ-21 give every tier a long-range strike option, and hypersonic glide shortens warning time.
- **Layered, abundant air defense.** A full HQ ladder, new anti-drone systems and lasers/HPM mean China's air defense is deep and varied, not a single high-end asset.
- **Drone integration at every echelon.** Drone-carrying vehicles (Type 100 support), FPV/loitering munitions, UGVs, robot dogs, GJ-11 wingmen, and the Jiu Tian mothership for swarming; "mass and cheap" is the doctrine (backed by 2026 Iran-war lessons).
- **Networked kill chain.** 500+ ISR satellites, BeiDou, EW aircraft and cyber units aim to find and fix forces at range; "systems destruction" targets nodes.
- **Industrial mass and mobilization.** Wartime-footing defense industry, huge shipbuilding capacity, and militia/fishing fleets for blockade/screening give depth and surge.
- **Nuclear backdrop.** Rapid arsenal growth and launch-on-warning posture constrain escalation options.

**Vulnerabilities**
- **Untested.** No peer combat since 1979; parade hardware is pre-series or unproven; many systems seen once.
- **Network dependence.** The kill chain rests on satellites, data links and sensors; counter-space, EW, and cyber attacks cripple coordination (see AI-targeting data-quality worries in PLA commentary).
- **Directed-energy limits.** Lasers are weather/smoke sensitive, power-limited and slow to engage; HPM claims have ~3 km reach; may not be in service.
- **Heavy, expensive new armor.** Type 100 is a high-tech, costly platform; numbers likely limited early; hybrid and APS claims unverified.
- **Mothership and large UAV signature.** Jiu Tian and GJ-11 are slow/large, vulnerable to air defense and fighters; swarm release range limited.
- **Amphibious and logistics gaps.** Landing is the hardest campaign per the Pentagon; blockade barrages by militia are exposed to being broken.
- **Leadership turmoil and corruption.** Rocket Force and nuclear-industry purges; reorganizations (SSF split) may degrade integration.
- **Cost exchange pressure in air defense.** PLA's own studies concede interceptor shortages and limited coverage.
